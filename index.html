Flight Control System (FCS) Architecture

The Baywing EF-150 utilizes a closed-loop, fly-by-wire Flight Control System (FCS) running at 100 Hz. The architecture integrates multi-sensor fusion with active T-foil flap actuation to maintain altitude, roll, pitch, and yaw stabilization across sea state variations without pilot fatigue.

1. Sensor Payload & Perception

  • Optical & LiDAR Profilers: Dual forward-looking LiDAR and ultrasonic sensors measuring incoming wave height at 200 Hz to pre-adjust foil angles.
  • Triple-Redundant IMU: Aviation-grade MEMS IMUs providing real-time 6-DOF pitch, roll, heave, and yaw data.
  • Hydro-Speed Tracking: Dual Pitot-static and Doppler Velocity Log (DVL) for precise speed and drift monitoring.

2. Control Loops & Modes

  • Active Ride Control (ARC): PID + Model Predictive Control (MPC) algorithms dynamically adjust foil deflections 100 times per second.
  • Wake Suppression Mode: Automated envelope restriction in harbors, locking wake wash profile below 0.15 meters.
  • Autonomous Transitions: Automated foil-borne takeoff and landing sequences between 16 and 22 knots.

3. Safety & Redundancy

  • Flight Computers: 2 Active / 1 Hot-Standby computer architecture with voter logic ensures zero single-point failure.
  • Fail-Passive Actuation: Servo actuators feature mechanical return springs to feather flaps to neutral lift upon pressure loss.
  • Classification: Designed to DNV High Speed Light Craft (HSLC) rules and ISO 26262 functional safety standards (HKMD conformant).

Grid-Independent Shore Power Solution

Floating Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) barges act as buffer reservoirs, mitigating high grid connection demand charges and enabling rapid Megawatt Charging System (MCS) throughput during turnarounds.
